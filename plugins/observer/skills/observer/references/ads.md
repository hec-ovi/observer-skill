# Ads in the transcript

The user marked this video as carrying ads. The transcript therefore contains stretches
that are not the talk: sponsor reads, promo codes, "this episode is brought to you by",
merch plugs, subscription pitches, and the host's own trailers for other episodes. The
transcriber cannot tell them apart from the content. You can.

## How to spot them

They read differently from the rest of the video: second person, an offer, a URL or a
code, a discount, a deadline, a switch to marketing cadence, a product praised without
technical detail. They cluster at the start, before a topic change, and near the end. A
sponsor segment usually opens and closes with a hard seam, the speaker snapping back into
the argument mid-thought.

Ordinary technical mentions of a product are not ads. A researcher saying which GPU they
trained on is content. The same name inside an offer is an ad.

## What to do with them

- Never write a concept, a definition, or a note from ad copy.
- Never attribute an ad's claim to the speaker as a position they hold.
- Never build a visual from an ad's numbers. Those numbers are marketing and they will be
  wrong in a way you cannot check.
- Do not count ad time as part of a concept's range. A concept that spans an ad break has
  a gap in the middle, not a claim about a sponsor.

## If the user pauses inside one

Say what it is in one short line and give them the nearest real content: what the segment
before the break was building, or what the speaker returns to after it. Do not summarize
the ad, do not repeat the offer, do not read the code.

## When you are unsure

Treat a stretch as content unless the marketing signature is clear. Losing a real minute
costs the user an explanation; keeping a suspect one costs a sentence.
